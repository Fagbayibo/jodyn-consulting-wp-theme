## 2. Signs you need an audit
Status: approved

Eyebrow: Warning signs
Heading: When It Is Time for a Systems Audit
Text: Most problems in systems and controls stay hidden until something goes wrong. If any of these sound familiar, an independent review will tell you where you stand.
- Numbers you cannot fully trust — Reports do not match, reconciliations take too long and nobody is sure which figure is right.
- Growth without controls — The business has grown, but approvals, checks and segregation of duties have not kept up.
- Work that depends on a few people — Key tasks live in someone's head or inbox, and everything stops when they are away.
- Systems that no longer fit — Software and spreadsheets were added over time, and now they overlap, break or leave data exposed.
- A loss, fraud or near miss — Something has already slipped through, and you need to know how and whether it could happen again.
- A regulator, investor or lender is asking questions — You need evidence that your controls and governance are sound.
Text: Finding these issues early costs far less than dealing with the consequences later.

## 4. How an audit works
Status: approved

Eyebrow: Our approach
Heading: How a Systems Audit Works
Text: Every audit follows a clear, structured process with as little disruption to your team as possible. You will always know what we are reviewing and why.
- Scope — We agree what will be reviewed, the key risks and questions, the timeline and who we will speak to. You receive: an agreed audit scope and plan.
- Review & Test — We interview your team, walk through your processes and test your controls and systems against the agreed scope. You receive: regular updates and early warning of anything urgent.
- Report — We present our findings, rated by risk, with clear and practical recommendations. You receive: an audit report with a prioritised action plan.
- Follow Up — We check progress on the agreed actions and help you close the gaps. You receive: a follow-up review confirming what has been fixed and what remains.

- Is a systems audit the same as a statutory financial audit? — No. A statutory audit gives an opinion on your financial statements. A systems audit looks at how your processes, controls and systems work, so you can reduce risk and run more efficiently. [PLACEHOLDER: confirm whether JoDyn also offers statutory financial audits.]
- How long does a systems audit take? — It depends on the scope and the size of your organisation. A focused review of one area can take one to two weeks, while a wider review takes several weeks. We agree the timeline before any work begins.
- How much does an audit cost? — Fees depend on the scope, size and complexity of the review. After an initial conversation, we send a clear proposal with the scope, deliverables, timeline and fees.
- Will the audit disrupt our work? — We plan interviews and testing around your team's schedule and keep requests to what the review needs.
- Will our information be kept confidential? — Yes. Everything you share is treated with strict confidentiality and used only for the audit. We are happy to sign a non-disclosure agreement.
- Can you help us fix the issues you find? — Yes. Our advisory and training teams can help you put the recommendations in place, or you can implement them yourselves using the action plan.
