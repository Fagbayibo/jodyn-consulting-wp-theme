Both formats work, for different goals. This guide helps you choose between classroom, online and hybrid training for your team, and get the most from whichever you pick.

## The Question Has Changed

A few years ago, most corporate training in Nigeria happened in a room. Today, many organisations run training online, in person or a mix of both, and staff are comfortable with all three.

So the question is no longer whether online training works. It is which format suits the skills you are building, the people you are training and the constraints you are working with.

## When In-Person Training Works Best

Classroom training is at its strongest when learning depends on people practising together.

- **Interpersonal skills:** leadership, negotiation, customer service and difficult conversations improve most through role-play, observation and immediate feedback.
- **Team building:** when the aim is for a team to work better together, being in the same room matters.
- **Complex or sensitive topics:** people often speak more openly, and ask more questions, face to face.
- **Fewer distractions:** time away from the desk helps participants focus fully on learning.

The trade-offs are cost and time: venue, travel and accommodation for staff in other cities, and longer time away from work.

## When Online Training Works Best

Live online training is often the better choice when:

- **Teams are spread out:** staff in Lagos, Abuja and Port Harcourt can learn together without travelling.
- **The content is knowledge-based:** policies, processes, systems and technical updates translate well to a screen.
- **Time is tight:** shorter sessions spread over several days fit around work more easily than full days away.
- **Budget matters:** without venue and travel costs, more people can be trained for the same spend.

The trade-offs are attention and interaction. Online sessions need more deliberate design to keep people engaged, and they depend on reliable internet and power.

## When Hybrid Makes Sense

Hybrid training combines the two. It can mean some participants in the room while others join online, or a programme that mixes formats over time: for example, online sessions to cover the knowledge, followed by an in-person day to practise and apply it.

The second approach often gives the best of both, because each format is used for what it does best. The first, mixed audiences in a single session, needs careful facilitation so that online participants are not left as observers.

## Five Questions to Help You Choose

1. **What should people be able to do afterwards?** If it is a behaviour or interpersonal skill, lean towards in-person or hybrid. If it is knowledge, online works well.
2. **Where are your participants?** One location favours in-person; several cities favour online.
3. **How much time can they spend away from work?** Full days suit in-person; short sessions suit online.
4. **What is your budget per person?** Include travel and time away, not just the training fee.
5. **What is the connectivity like?** For online training, make sure every participant has reliable internet, power and a quiet place to join from.

## Making Either Format Work

The format matters less than the design. Whichever you choose:

- **Keep groups small enough to interact.** Learning happens through discussion and practice, not just presentation.
- **Use real examples** from your organisation, so people can apply what they learn.
- **Brief managers beforehand,** so they know what their staff are learning and can support them afterwards.
- **Online, keep cameras on and sessions short,** with practical activities every 20 to 30 minutes.
- **In person, protect the time:** no laptops open on email, and no calls back to the office.
- **Follow up:** a short check-in or refresher a few weeks later makes the learning stick.

## Key takeaways

- Choose the format based on what people need to be able to do afterwards.
- In-person suits interpersonal skills, team building and sensitive topics.
- Online suits knowledge, spread-out teams, tight schedules and tight budgets.
- Hybrid works well when each format is used for what it does best.
- Good design and follow-up matter more than the format itself.
